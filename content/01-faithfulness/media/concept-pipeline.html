A confident answer arrives

The assistant answers a question about a listing and it comes through as one stream — you either take the whole thing or you don't. Part of it isn't in the documents it retrieved, and nothing downstream is looking for that yet.

The problem
Grounded question answering

Put instruments on the line

Instead of judging the answer as a whole, three steps run in order: break it into separate statements, check each one, then total up how many held. That gives you something to measure — not yet a number worth trusting.

From RAGAS
The evaluation loop

Split it into single facts

“It has two bedrooms and a gym” looks like one statement but it is two, and only one might be true. Checked together, half a mistake passes as a pass. Split apart, each fact has to stand on its own.

From FActScore
One fact at a time

Block what the documents don't back

Every strand is checked against the retrieved documents only — not against general knowledge. A fact can be true in the real world and still get blocked here, because the system had no source for it. What gets through, over what went in, is the score.

The measurement
Supported over total

Aim it at failures that really happen

A price that's wrong. Details from two different flats blended into one. A feature nobody mentioned. Nothing retrieved at all. Eight specific ways this breaks in a property assistant — and the line has to survive every one.

Not from any paper

Then check the checker

The thing doing the grading is itself a machine making judgement calls, so you grade it too. Put its reading next to a person's on the same cases. On the eight tested they match. Past that point the line keeps running and nobody has measured it.
